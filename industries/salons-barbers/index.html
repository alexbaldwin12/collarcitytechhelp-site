Keep your chairs full and your client list safe

By Alex Baldwin, Collar City Tech Help, Troy, NY. Last updated 2026-10-08.

A salon or barbershop runs on trust and a full book. Behind the scenes that book is usually a booking app, a phone with client numbers, a few social accounts and an email inbox. If any of those fall into the wrong hands, you feel it the same day.

Booths get rented, stylists come and go, and the front-desk tablet gets passed around all week. The security side rarely gets attention because the schedule always comes first. A short, honest check can show you what is worth ten minutes.

What the Security Scorecard checks for salons and barbershops

The Security Scorecard is a $79 report based on a 10-minute online questionnaire plus public checks on your business email domain. Your answers shape the advice, so a solo barber with one chair sees a different fix list than a salon with rented booths.

  • Booking app and front-desk login. We ask whether staff share one login and whether two-step login is on. If everyone uses the same sign-in, you cannot tell who changed an appointment or exported a list. The report suggests a simple fix.
  • Client phone numbers and email lists. Your answer about storing customer information tells us how much is at stake. The report points to where lists tend to end up, like personal phones and old spreadsheets, and how to tidy that.
  • Instagram, Facebook and Google listing access. A hacked social page can send fake messages to your clients. We ask about two-step login on social accounts, and the report puts that near the top if it is off.
  • Passwords that get reused. When one salon-supply or booking password is reused elsewhere, one leak can open several doors. The report tells you if a password manager is worth it for your size.
  • Backups of photos, price lists and records. Before-and-after photos and client notes are work you cannot redo. We ask if backups run automatically and if you ever tested a restore, then explain what to set up.
  • Guest Wi-Fi and the router. Clients wait with phones in hand. We ask if guests share your work network and whether the router default password was changed. The report says if a guest network is worth setting up.
  • Email domain protection: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX. We look up these public records for your business email. The report explains whether someone could easily send email that looks like it came from your shop, and what to ask your email provider.

Problems we hear about in this line of work

The stylist who left still has access

When someone leaves, their booking calendar, shared logins and social posting rights can stay active for months. A simple checklist for when someone starts or goes closes that gap.

Client details living on personal phones

Numbers, notes and photos pile up in texts and camera rolls. If a phone is lost or sold, that information goes with it. A screen lock and automatic backup help a great deal.

A fake message that looks like your booking app

Scam emails often claim your account is suspended or a payout is waiting. They are designed to make you click fast. Slowing down and going to the app directly, not through the email, is the habit that protects you.

Three quick wins you can do this week

  1. Give each stylist or barber their own login in your booking app if it allows it, and remove anyone who no longer works with you.
  2. Turn on two-step login for your Instagram, Facebook and business email. Use an authenticator app or the text option, whichever you will actually use.
  3. Put a screen lock on the front-desk tablet and any phone that holds client contacts, and switch on automatic updates.
