Closing-day email security for real estate agents

By Alex, founder, Collar City Tech Help, Troy, NY. Last updated 2026-10-08.

A transaction runs on email. Offers, disclosures, title instructions and wiring details all arrive in an inbox, usually while you are driving between showings. Everyone involved trusts what lands there.

Scammers aim at that trust. A message that looks like it came from your title company, with new wiring instructions, can move fast. Your logins for the MLS, your lockbox app and your CRM matter too. This page explains how the Scorecard looks at all of it.

What the Security Scorecard checks for real estate agents

The Security Scorecard is a $79 report. You answer a 10-minute online questionnaire and we run public checks on your email domain. You get a graded report with a fix list. The advice follows your answers, so a solo agent and a small team with a transaction coordinator get different advice.

  • Spoofed closing and wire-instruction emails. We ask about suspicious payment requests and who can approve changes to payment details. The report explains a simple rule: wire instructions are always confirmed by phone with a number you already know.
  • Two-step login on your email. Your inbox is the key to every transaction. If it is taken over, a scammer can watch deals and reply as you. If it is off, the report ranks it high.
  • Shared transaction email and team inboxes. Team and coordinator mailboxes often share one login. Questions about shared logins show where each person should have their own sign-in.
  • MLS, lockbox and CRM app logins. We ask about password reuse, password managers and who has access. The report names what to tighten so a single leaked password does not open every app.
  • Phones, laptops and open-house devices. Agents work from phones in cars and tablets at open houses. We ask about screen locks and automatic updates, and the report flags the weakest devices first.
  • Backups of contracts and client files. Signed documents need to be there when you need them. The questionnaire asks if backups run on their own and whether you have tested a restore.
  • Email domain protection: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX. These public records show whether someone could easily send mail that looks like it came from your brokerage or personal domain. The report explains the results in plain English and tells you what to ask your email provider.

Problems we hear about in this line of work

New wiring instructions, same thread

A message arrives inside what looks like the normal conversation, with a small change to the account number. The fix is a habit. Call to confirm, using a number from earlier in the file.

A taken-over inbox

If someone gets into your email, they can read open deals and send convincing messages. Two-step login on the email account is among the most useful changes you can make.

Free email on a business card

Using a free address for client work is common and fine to start with. A custom domain with the right records set up makes you harder to imitate. The report explains where you stand.

Three quick wins for this week

  1. Add a line to your email signature and client onboarding: wire instructions will never change by email, and clients should call you to confirm.
  2. Turn on two-step login for your email, MLS-connected tools and phone cloud account.
  3. Give team members their own logins instead of sharing one for the transaction inbox.
