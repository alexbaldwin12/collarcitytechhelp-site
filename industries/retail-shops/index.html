Security for shops with a counter, a website, or both

By Alex Baldwin, Collar City Tech Help, Troy, NY. Last updated 2026-10-08.

Retail has more moving parts than it looks. There is a register, a stock list, maybe an online store, a supplier inbox full of invoices, and a handful of staff who all need to ring things up. Holiday help makes it busier still.

When something goes wrong in a shop, it is usually ordinary. A password that gets reused. A gift card scam at the counter. A supplier email that is not quite from the supplier. Knowing where yours is exposed is the first step.

What the Security Scorecard checks for retail shops

The Security Scorecard is a $79 report. You complete a 10-minute online questionnaire, we run public checks on your business email domain, and you receive a graded report with a fix list. The advice follows what you tell us, so a one-person boutique and a shop with a team get different next steps.

  • Register and POS logins. We ask whether staff share one login and whether devices lock when unattended. The report shows where separate sign-ins would help you see who did what at the till.
  • Online store admin accounts. If you sell online, one stolen admin password can change prices or redirect payouts. Questions on password reuse and two-step login show how well that door is guarded.
  • Gift card and refund scams. Scammers push clerks to sell large gift cards or refund to a different card. The questionnaire covers staff training on scams, and the report suggests a short counter script.
  • Vendor email spoofing and invoice changes. We ask about suspicious invoices and who approves payment changes. Supplier mail is easy to imitate, and the report covers confirming new bank details by phone.
  • Seasonal staff and shared devices. Holiday hires need access fast, and it often stays on afterward. Your answers about shared logins and who has access help the report suggest a start and end routine.
  • Backups of inventory and sales records. A broken laptop should not erase your stock list. We ask if backups run automatically and whether you tested a restore, and the report names what to set up.
  • Email domain protection: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX. We look up these public records for your shop's email domain. The report explains whether a scammer could easily send mail that looks like yours, which customers and suppliers might then trust.

Problems we hear about in this line of work

Same password, many shop accounts

The same password for email, the online store and the supplier portal means one leak opens all three. A password manager is the usual fix, and some good ones are free.

The big gift card sale

A customer in a hurry wants a lot of gift cards, and is on the phone with someone urging them on. Staff who know to pause and ask a few questions can stop a bad day for everyone.

A supplier email that is slightly off

A changed letter in the address or a new payment instruction can be hard to spot. A rule to verify changes by calling a saved number is more reliable than trying to spot the fake.

Three quick wins you can do this week

  1. List everyone with admin access to your online store and register system, and remove or downgrade the ones who do not need it.
  2. Turn on two-step login for your business email, online store and bank. Start with email.
  3. Print a few lines for the counter about gift card scams and what to do if a customer seems pressured.
