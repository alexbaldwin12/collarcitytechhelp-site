Security for contractors who run the office from a truck

By Alex Baldwin, Collar City Tech Help, Troy, NY. Last updated 2026-10-08.

Your office is a phone, a laptop and whatever email app is open at a red light. Estimates go out from a job site. Invoices go out at night. The crew shares a phone or two, and photos of finished work sit in a camera roll.

That setup moves fast, which is the point. It also makes you a good target for the fake invoice that looks like a supplier's, or the email that says the bank details changed. A little structure goes a long way.

What the Security Scorecard checks for contractors and trades

The Security Scorecard is a $79 report. You answer a 10-minute online questionnaire, we run public checks on your business email domain, and you receive a graded report with a fix list in order. The advice follows your answers, so a two-person crew and a ten-person shop get different next steps.

  • Estimates and invoices sent by email. When customers pay from an emailed invoice, someone can copy it and change the bank details. We ask about suspicious payment requests and the report explains simple habits, like confirming changes by a separate call.
  • Who can approve payments and bank changes. We ask whether one person or two must approve changing payment details. The report shows how a second set of eyes stops the most common fake-invoice trick.
  • QuickBooks and bank logins. Questions about password reuse and two-step login on bank and payment accounts show how well the money side is protected. If it is off, the report ranks turning it on near the top.
  • Shared phones and tablets on the crew. We ask about shared logins and screen locks. A phone left in a truck that opens straight to email is an easy problem to fix once you see it.
  • Job-site photos, plans and contracts backup. Photos and signed documents are proof of what you did. The questionnaire asks if backups run on their own and whether you tested restoring, and the report names what to set up if not.
  • Old phones, laptops and updates. We ask if devices update automatically and if any run unsupported systems. The report lists which ones to replace or update first.
  • Email domain protection: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX. These public records show whether others could easily send mail that looks like it came from your company. That matters when customers pay based on what lands in their inbox.

Problems we hear about in this line of work

The invoice with new bank details

A supplier or customer email says the account has changed, please pay here. It can look exactly right. The safe move is to call a number you already have on file before sending anything.

One phone, many hands

A shared job phone is logged in to email, the invoicing app and maybe your bank. If it is lost or left on a tailgate, a lot goes with it. A lock code and the right level of access cut that risk.

Photos that live in one place

If your only copy of job photos and permits is on one phone, a drop in a puddle ends the record. Automatic cloud or drive backup is usually free to start.

A fake estimate request

Strange links or attachments sent as a quote request are a classic. Opening them on the same device where you do your banking is the risk to avoid.

Three quick wins you can do this week

  1. Pick one rule this week: any change to payment details gets confirmed by calling a known number first.
  2. Turn on two-step login for your email, QuickBooks and bank. Do the email first, since resets go there.
  3. Set photos on every work phone to back up automatically, and give each phone a lock code.
