Security for contractors who run the office from a truck

By Alex, founder, Collar City Tech Help, Troy, NY. Last updated 2026-10-08.

Your office is a phone, a laptop and whatever email app is open at a red light. Estimates go out from a job site. Invoices go out at night. The crew shares a phone or two, and photos of finished work sit in a camera roll.

That setup moves fast, which is the point. It also makes you a good target for a fake invoice that looks like a supplier's, or an email saying the bank details changed. A little structure helps.

What the Security Scorecard checks for contractors and trades

The Security Scorecard is a $79 report. You answer a 10-minute online questionnaire and we run public checks on your business email domain. You get a graded report with a fix list in order. The advice follows your answers, so a two-person crew and a ten-person shop get different next steps.

  • Estimates and invoices sent by email. When customers pay from an emailed invoice, someone can copy it and change the bank details. We ask about suspicious payment requests, and the report suggests simple habits like confirming changes with a separate call.
  • Who can approve payments and bank changes. We ask whether one person or two must approve changing payment details. The report shows how a second set of eyes can stop the most common fake-invoice trick.
  • QuickBooks and bank logins. Questions about password reuse and two-step login on bank and payment accounts show how well the money side is protected. If two-step login is off, the report puts turning it on near the top.
  • Shared phones and tablets on the crew. We ask about shared logins and screen locks. A phone left in a truck that opens straight to email is easy to fix once you spot it.
  • Job-site photos, plans and contracts backup. Photos and signed documents are proof of what you did. The questionnaire asks if backups run on their own and whether you have tested a restore. If not, the report names what to set up.
  • Old phones, laptops and updates. We ask if devices update automatically and if any run unsupported systems. The report lists which ones to replace or update first.
  • Email domain protection: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX. These public records show whether someone could easily send mail that looks like it came from your company. That matters when customers pay based on what lands in their inbox.

Problems we hear about in this line of work

The invoice with new bank details

A supplier or customer email says the account has changed and to pay there. It can look exactly right. Call a number you already have on file before you send anything.

One phone, many hands

A shared job phone is often logged in to email, the invoicing app and maybe your bank. If it is lost or left on a tailgate, a lot goes with it. A lock code and limited access cut that risk.

Photos that live in one place

If your only copy of job photos and permits is on one phone, a drop in a puddle ends the record. Automatic cloud or drive backup is usually free to start.

A fake estimate request

Odd links or attachments sent as a quote request are a classic. Avoid opening them on the same device where you do your banking.

Three quick wins for this week

  1. Pick one rule: any change to payment details gets confirmed by calling a known number first.
  2. Turn on two-step login for your email, QuickBooks and bank. Start with email, since password resets go there.
  3. Set photos on every work phone to back up automatically, and give each phone a lock code.
