Fleet account

One account. Every unit. One statement.

Everything a fleet manager asks for, in one place: your vehicles, your purchase orders, one invoice a month and bay time held for you.

  • Every vehicle on file

    Unit number, plate, VIN, mileage and the full service history, kept against the vehicle and exportable.

  • PO numbers on every job

    Add a purchase-order number when a unit is booked. It rides on the work order and prints on the invoice.

  • Monthly invoicing

    One statement a month, itemized by unit and PO, with net terms for approved accounts (sample terms).

  • Priority bays

    Reserved bay hours and a named advisor. A unit that is down moves ahead of walk-ins.

What an account includes

Six things that keep vehicles earning.

  • Priority scheduling

    A named service advisor and a standing weekly window. Vehicles on your account move ahead of walk-ins when a unit is down.

  • Preventive-maintenance schedules

    Mileage- or hours-based intervals per unit, with reminders by text or email before a unit is due.

  • Service history by unit

    Every inspection, photo and invoice kept against the vehicle and unit number, exportable on request.

  • Consolidated billing

    One monthly statement. Net terms are available to approved accounts.

  • Pickup, delivery and after-hours drop

    Key drop box, shuttle within a set radius, or pickup and return from your yard.

  • Inspection and compliance support

    Pre-trip and annual inspection records, state inspection where it applies. We do not give legal advice on regulations.

How a fleet account runs

From first call to quarterly review.

  1. 1

    Account setup

    One conversation about your vehicles, mileage, routes and uptime goals. We build the unit list and the maintenance schedule from it.

  2. 2

    Scheduled windows

    Recurring slots and a named advisor. Units arrive on schedule and leave the same day wherever the work allows.

  3. 3

    Inspect, report, approve

    Every visit produces a photo inspection report. Repairs over your approval limit wait for your sign-off; routine items run on pre-approval.

  4. 4

    Statement and review

    One monthly statement by unit. A quarterly review covers cost per vehicle, repeat issues and what is coming due.

Fleet questions

Operations, answered.

What counts as a fleet?

Sample answer: five or more vehicles under one account, from a contractor with a few vans to a municipal or rental fleet. Smaller businesses can still open a commercial account for priority scheduling and one statement.

Do you service medium-duty trucks?

Sample answer: light and medium duty up to the capacity of our lifts, by quote. Heavy-duty work is referred out and we say so before you drive in.

Can you work on our schedule?

Yes. Early drop-off, after-hours key box and recurring windows are standard for accounts. Your real hours and capacity go here.

Do you provide DOT or inspection paperwork?

We keep inspection records and perform inspections where they apply. We do not give legal advice on regulations; your compliance officer owns the rules.
