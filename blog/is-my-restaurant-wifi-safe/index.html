Is My Restaurant Wifi Safe? A Yes/No Self-Check

By Alex, founder, Collar City Tech Help. Published October 9, 2026.

Most restaurant and cafe owners never planned to manage a network. An installer or a helpful relative set up the wifi during opening week, it worked, and nobody touched it again. This is a quick quiz you can run on your own place. Answer yes or no to each of the eight questions. Every no comes with a next step. It is general guidance, not legal advice, and it cannot tell you whether you meet any card industry rule.

If you want the full setup steps for a guest network, our guide to restaurant wifi security and guest networks goes step by step. This post is the short check you can do on a slow afternoon. For router advice we use CISA's public guidance, Securing Your Home Wi-Fi. It is written for homes, but the router basics apply to a small shop too.

Question 1: Do guests use a different wifi network than your staff and equipment?

Yes: good. No: log in to your router and look for a setting called Guest Network, Guest Wi-Fi or Guest SSID. CISA recommends turning it on if your router has one. Give it its own name and its own password. If your router has no guest option, ask your internet provider about a newer router.

Question 2: Is the register or card reader set up the way your payment provider says?

Answer yes only if you have actually checked. Payment setups differ. Some devices use cellular, some use wifi and some use a cable. Ask your payment provider which connection they expect, and write the answer down. Do not move a payment device to a different network just because it seems tidier. Also think about who can reach the register. If customers can join the same network the register uses, call your provider.

Question 3: Did you change the router's admin password from the one on the label?

The wifi password and the router admin password are two different things. The admin password controls the router's settings. CISA says to change the default login to one that is long, random and unique. If it is still the label password, change it today and keep the new one in a password manager. Our post on password managers for small businesses shows how.

Question 4: Does your wifi use WPA3 or WPA2 with AES?

Look in the router's wireless settings under Security or Encryption. CISA recommends WPA3 Personal or WPA2 AES, and says open networks, WEP, WPA and WPA2 TKIP are not considered secure. If yours shows one of those, ask your provider for an upgrade or consider a new router.

Question 5: Is remote management turned off?

Remote management lets someone change your router's settings from the internet. Most small restaurants do not need it, and CISA recommends turning it off. If your internet provider or IT person uses it to support you, ask before changing the setting, and ask why it is on.

Question 6: Do the tablets, cameras and other smart gadgets each have their own login, and do you know who has it?

Think of the order tablet, the music streamer, the security camera app and the smart thermostat. First, check that none of them still uses its factory username and password. Second, find out who can log in. A former employee who still knows the camera password is a common gap. CISA suggests putting smart devices that only need internet access, like a music speaker or a TV, on the guest network so they cannot reach other equipment. Cameras are different. Check the maker's instructions, since some apps need to reach a recorder on your private side. If you are unsure, ask the maker or installer.

Question 7: Is the router and everything else up to date?

Look in the router's settings for a Firmware or Software Update section. CISA advises checking for updates regularly, and some routers can update on their own. Check the office computer and any tablet used for orders too, since each one has its own updates. If your router is very old and the maker no longer sends updates, replace it. Our backup guide is worth a look before you update anything important.

Question 8: Do you know every device on your network?

Most routers show a list of connected devices. Scroll through it. You should recognize the register, the tablet, the office computer and the printer. If you see something odd, it may be a guest device that landed on the wrong network, or something old nobody removed. Unplug a device you think it might be and see whether the entry disappears. If you still cannot place it, ask your internet provider for help. After you change the wifi password, reconnect only the things you know.

How did you score?

  • 7 or 8 yeses: nice work. Put a reminder in your calendar to repeat this check twice a year.
  • 4 to 6 yeses: pick the first no and fix it this week. Questions 1, 3 and 5 are quick fixes.
  • 3 or fewer: start with the router admin password and the guest network, then work down the list. You do not need special skills for any of it.

Yes on every line will not make a place unhackable, but it closes several easy openings.
