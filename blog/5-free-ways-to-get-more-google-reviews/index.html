5 Free Ways to Get More Google Reviews for Your Small Business

By Alex, founder, Collar City Tech Help. Designed and built by Alex using modern tools, including AI. Published October 8, 2026.

When someone in Troy or the Capital Region searches for a local business, Google reviews are often what they read before they call. Getting more of them costs nothing. Here are five ways to do it, all within Google's rules.

Google changes its policies from time to time, so check the current rules in your Google Business Profile help pages. Never write fake reviews, and never pay for them.

1. Get your direct review link

In your Google Business Profile, open the option to get more reviews and copy the short link. Anyone who opens it lands on a box where they can write a review. Save the link in your phone notes so you can send it any time.

2. Ask at the right moment

Ask right after someone thanks you, or right after you finish a job. A short text works:

"Hi [name], thanks again for choosing us. If you have a minute, an honest Google review really helps a small local business like ours. Here's the link: [your link]. Thank you!"

It asks for an honest review and doesn't mention five stars. Do not offer discounts or gifts in exchange for a review, and do not only ask people you think will be happy. Google does not allow either.

3. Make it easy with a QR code

Turn your review link into a QR code using a free generator, then print it on a receipt, counter card, or invoice. Customers scan it with their phone camera and land on the review box. Scan it yourself before you print a hundred copies.

4. Reply to every review

Thank people who say nice things, and answer critical reviews calmly and briefly. Do not argue or share private details. Replies show future customers that a real person is reading, and many owners find that more people write reviews after that.

5. Ask a few people every week, not everyone once

A steady trickle of fresh reviews looks more natural than a burst of twenty in one day. Pick a small habit, like asking two or three happy customers each week, and stick with it.

A quick safety note

Scammers like to pose as review requests. Be careful with emails or texts that say your Google Business Profile will be suspended unless you click a link, or that offer to sell you reviews. Go to your profile directly instead of clicking. Our guide on how to spot a fake invoice scam has the same warning signs.
