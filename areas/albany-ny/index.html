Businesses we hear about most in Albany

  • Law, lobbying and consulting offices. With the Capitol and Empire State Plaza nearby, Albany has many small firms handling sensitive client documents and email all day. A single wrong click can matter a lot in that line of work.
  • Lunch spots and coffee counters. Downtown and the State Street corridor run on weekday lunch traffic from state workers. These shops lean on online ordering, delivery apps and card readers.
  • Lark Street and Center Square shops. The Lark Street district is known for independent restaurants, bars, boutiques and salons. Many owners handle their own websites and social pages.
  • Pine Hills and Central Avenue businesses. Central Avenue and the Pine Hills area near the colleges have service businesses, small groceries, tutoring and fitness studios that depend on a steady student and resident base.
  • Medical and dental practices. Albany Medical Center and other hospitals bring independent specialists, dentists and therapists to the surrounding neighborhoods. Their patient scheduling and email habits deserve a careful look.
  • Real estate and property managers. Albany has plenty of rental housing and older buildings. Small property managers and agents juggle tenant emails, wire instructions and shared documents.

Common tech and security problems for Albany businesses

Email scams aimed at offices

Offices that move money or documents often receive fake invoices and requests that look like they come from a boss or a client. Setting up basic email protections and a pause-and-confirm habit goes a long way.

Legislative-season rushes

Many Albany businesses get busy when the legislature is in session, roughly January through June. Rushed staff are more likely to click a fake link, so a short staff checklist helps before the busy months.

Public Wi-Fi and shared screens

Consultants and lawyers work from cafes, trains and borrowed desks. Using a trusted connection and locking screens are small habits that protect client files.

Too many accounts, too few owners

A small Albany office may use six or seven cloud tools, and one person set them all up years ago. We help you list them and decide who really needs access.
