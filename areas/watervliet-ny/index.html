Businesses we hear about most in Watervliet

  • Corner restaurants, bakeries and delis. Neighborhood food businesses along the main streets serve regulars and workers heading to and from the Arsenal and nearby offices.
  • Barbershops, salons and nail studios. Appointment-based shops take bookings by text, phone and social media. Customer names and numbers end up scattered across a few apps.
  • Auto repair and trades. Garages, plumbers, electricians and builders serve Watervliet and Colonie and Albany. Estimates and invoices go out by email, which makes fake-invoice scams a real concern.
  • Small offices and tax preparers. Insurance agents, tax preparers and bookkeepers serve local families. They keep ID-type documents and income records that need extra care.
  • Contractors for the Arsenal area. Some small firms in the area do work connected to nearby federal and industrial employers. They send contracts and quotes by email and shared folders.
  • Home-based businesses. Many residents run side businesses such as catering, cleaning and crafts from home, selling through social media and marketplaces.

Common tech and security problems for Watervliet businesses

Everything runs through a phone

Bookings, payments and messages all land in one phone. If it is lost or its account is taken over, the business stalls. Backups and two-step sign-in cover most of that.

Fake invoices and payment requests

Trades and offices send and receive invoices all week. Scammers copy that pattern. A call-back habit for any change in payment details helps.

Tax-season paperwork

Preparers and bookkeepers handle sensitive documents from winter into April. Sending them by plain email is a common weak spot, and safer options exist.

Customers never see you online

Some Watervliet businesses have an old or missing Google listing. A neighbor searching for you finds nothing or wrong hours. Regular posts and a tidy listing fix that.
